## The Dropped Torch

The empire kills the shamans. But it can't kill the genome.

This is the central fact of the next several thousand years of human history, and everything in this section follows from it. The cultural infrastructure for identifying, training, and initiating neurodivergent specialists was systematically destroyed — not once, but repeatedly, across millennia, by successive waves of imperial expansion. But the archaic inheritance persists. The cognitive distribution that produces specialists continues to express itself in every generation, in every population, because it's encoded in DNA that is hundreds of thousands of years old and is not subject to imperial policy. The torch keeps getting dropped. And it keeps getting picked back up — by people who never read each other's notes, who have no idea that anyone has seen what they're seeing, who derive the same insights from first principles because the insights aren't cultural. They're perceptual. They arise from a nervous system that is calibrated to detect something real.

Before we trace that pattern through history, we need to make one distinction sharp.

---

### Religion Is Not Spirituality

In the vocabulary we've been developing, the distinction is precise: **religion** is the ontic capture of spiritual practice. It's what the specialist's phenomenal relationship to the invisible world *becomes* when it is standardized into doctrine and subordinated to institutional authority.

The mystic says: "I had an experience of the divine." This is a phenomenal report — an assertion made by a speaker who is aware of their own assertive role, who knows they are *interpreting* something that happened to them. The doctrine says: "God *is* love." This is an apodictic assertion — it uses the copula to project a relationship onto reality as if reality itself asserts it. Same words, completely different mode of consciousness. The mystic is speaking depositionally. The doctrine is speaking apodictically. And the entire institutional machinery of imperial religion exists to ensure that the apodictic version is the one that gets transmitted.

Spirituality, in the sense that matters for this essay, is the persistence of phenomenal practice *within or beneath ontic structures*. It's the living water that keeps flowing even after the empire paves over the spring. And it has survived — stubbornly, ingeniously, often at enormous personal cost — through a series of strategies that amount to *smuggling the specialist's perception through the imperial night in disguise*.

---

### The Quiet Carriers

The most successful smuggling vehicle was the monastery.

Consider what a monastery actually provides, stripped of its doctrinal framing:

**Withdrawal from ordinary social life.** The monastery gives the socially atypical person a sanctioned reason to live apart from the mainstream — exactly the structural separation that shamanic cultures provided for their specialists.

**Contemplative practice.** Chanting, repetitive prayer, meditation, liturgical cycles — rhythmic, repetitive sensory-motor activities performed daily for years. These are structurally identical to the induction technologies we identified in section two: the same neural entrainment that shamanic drumming produces, the same relationship to stimming, the same pathway to altered states of consciousness.

**A community of mutual recognition.** A gathering of people who *recognize each other's experience* — who can say "yes, I know what you're describing" when someone reports a mystical state. This is the initiation mentorship structure in miniature: elders who have been through it, peers who are going through it, a shared language for experiences that the outside world has no category for.

**An initiatory framework.** Novitiate, vows, the "dark night of the soul" — a structured, culturally recognized transformative crisis with a role waiting on the other side.

**A sanctioned social role.** "Monk," "nun," "contemplative" — identities that the empire tolerates because they look like religious obedience but actually function as specialist roles.

The empire doesn't destroy these institutions because it doesn't recognize what they're doing. It sees obedient religious servants performing useful functions: copying manuscripts, maintaining hospitals, praying for the salvation of souls. What's *actually* happening is that the specialist's perception is being preserved under cover of institutional piety. The monastery is a Trojan horse. The shaman is wearing a habit.

This pattern repeats across every major imperial religion. Christian monasticism. Buddhist monastic orders. Sufi *tariqas*. Kabbalistic study circles. Hindu ashrams. In each case, the institution nominally serves the religion — but its internal practice generates the same altered states, the same community recognition, the same initiatory transformation, the same phenomenal perception that the religion's doctrinal structure cannot accommodate and would suppress if it understood clearly what was happening.

And the mystic traditions that emerge within these containers — Meister Eckhart, Julian of Norwich, John of the Cross, Teresa of Ávila, Rumi, Ibn Arabi, the Baal Shem Tov, Dōgen, Milarepa — are *consistently in tension with the doctrinal mainstream*. Eckhart was tried for heresy. Al-Hallaj was executed. Teresa was investigated by the Inquisition.[^monasteries] The tension is structural: the phenomenal practitioner operating inside the ontic institution is always at risk of being recognized for what they are. The institution tolerates them as long as their reports can be *absorbed into doctrine*. The moment the mystic says something that the doctrine can't metabolize — "I and the Father are one," "I am the Truth," "there is no self" — the immune system activates.

Other smuggling routes existed alongside the monasteries. The *yurodiviy* (holy fool) tradition in Orthodox Christianity explicitly sanctioned specialist-type behavior — social transgression, truth-telling that no one wants to hear, apparent madness that carries prophetic insight — as a sacred category. Mystery schools, from the Eleusinian mysteries through the Hermetic traditions, preserved initiatory structures in forms the empire classified as philosophy or entertainment rather than spiritual competition. And artistic lineages — poetry, music, visual art — became vessels for phenomenal perception because the empire categorized aesthetics as *decorative* rather than *dangerous*. You could say things in a poem that would get you burned at the stake if you said them in a sermon.[^mystery_schools]

### The Founders

Here we need to address the elephants in the room: Jesus, Buddha, Moses, Mohammed. And Osiris, who may be the oldest of them all.

In our framework, these figures are not fundamentally different from the specialists we've been describing. They are people who made direct contact with phenomenal reality — who underwent initiatory crisis (Jesus in the desert for forty days, Buddha under the bodhi tree, Moses on the mountain, Mohammed in the cave) and came back with reports that shattered the ontic consensus of their time. They are shamanic specialists operating at scale.

The difference is timing. They emerged *before the empire had fully developed its immune system* against phenomenal eruption. Jesus appears in a Roman province where the local shamanic traditions (Jewish prophecy, Essene mysticism) are still partially intact and the imperial religious apparatus (Roman state religion) is sophisticated enough to administrate but not yet totalizing. Buddha appears in a Vedic culture that still has living connections to earlier phenomenal practice. In each case, the specialist's report propagates fast enough and widely enough that the empire can't simply kill it and move on.

So the empire does something more sophisticated than suppression: it *incorporates* the signal. It takes the phenomenal report and converts it into ontic infrastructure. The living water becomes plumbing. Jesus's direct experience of the divine becomes the Nicene Creed — a set of apodictic propositions ("We believe in one God... *begotten, not made, being of one substance with the Father*") that could hardly be more explicitly ontic if Dewart had written them as an example. Buddha's liberation from the wheel of suffering becomes institutional Buddhism with its hierarchies, its orthodoxies, its own imperial entanglements. Mohammed's revelation becomes the Caliphate. Moses's encounter on the mountain becomes Levitical law.

In each case, the pattern is the same: phenomenal experience → attempted transmission → ontic capture → the doctrine *becomes the empire*, and anyone who subsequently encounters the living phenomenal reality directly is a heretic, a deviant, a threat to the very institution that was built around a previous encounter with the same reality.

And this is where Ishmael Reed picks up the thread.

### Jes' Grew

In *Mumbo Jumbo* (1972), Reed tells the story we've been telling, but he tells it from a vantage point that the European intellectual tradition can't reach — because the European tradition is the empire, and the empire can't diagnose itself.[^reed1972]

Reed's mythology begins with Osiris, who learns "the arts of peace and plenty" from Ethiopian and Nubian teachers and disseminates this gnosis — this phenomenal, embodied, ecstatic knowledge — throughout the world. His brother Set, "the stick crook and flail man," advocates discipline and eventually founds the Atonist religion: the worship of Aton, the transcendent sun god. Set murders Osiris. Isis reassembles the pieces but one is missing — the generative organ, the capacity for the tradition to reproduce itself. Osiris can rule the underworld but cannot return fully to the living world.

This is, in our framework, a myth about the destruction of specialist infrastructure. The phenomenal tradition (Osiris) is dismembered by the ontic order (Set/Aton). It survives in the underworld (underground, in disguise, in the margins) but cannot reproduce itself at scale because the *sociocultural matrix* — the community of transmission — has been severed. The missing piece is not a body part. It's the initiation lineage.

Reed traces the Atonist order forward through history — through Moses (who, in Reed's counter-history, "swindled the secrets of Osiris" and got only the negative side, which became monotheism), through Christianity, through European colonialism — as a single continuous operation of ontic capture. And he names the force that the Atonist order is trying to suppress: **Jes' Grew**.

Jes' Grew is Reed's name for the phenomenal vitality that keeps erupting despite suppression. It's an "anti-plague" — it doesn't make you sick, it makes you *dance*. It makes you *feel*. It erupts in ragtime, in jazz, in blues, in rock and roll. Its symptoms are joy, embodiment, rhythmic entrainment, community bonding, and the dissolution of the rigid categories that the ontic order depends on. In the language of this essay, Jes' Grew is the specialist's perception — the groove — erupting at population scale through the cultural forms that preserved it.

And here is what makes Reed's contribution indispensable to our argument: he makes explicit what the European lineage of Blake/Jung/PKD cannot see about itself, because it *is* the imperial culture. **The racialized dimension of the pattern is not incidental. It is structural.**

If Dewart is right that ontic consciousness is a property of Indo-European language and culture, then the empire's violence falls disproportionately on non-IE peoples *by definition*, because they are the carriers of phenomenal consciousness in its most intact forms. The Atlantic slave trade was not just an economic atrocity — it was the most massive, systematic destruction of phenomenal cultural infrastructure in recorded history. Entire civilizations with living specialist traditions — Yoruba, Fon, Kongo, Akan — were shattered, their people scattered across the Americas, their practitioners killed or silenced.

And what happened? The tradition survived anyway. Vodou, Candomblé, Santería, Hoodoo — these are the pieces of Osiris, reassembled under impossible conditions. The specialists encoded their knowledge in songs, in rhythms, in syncretic saint-worship that the Catholic masters couldn't parse. They smuggled the phenomenal through the ontic using the same strategy as the monasteries, but under conditions of unimaginably greater violence and with incomparably greater success. Because the music that came out of that survival — spirituals into blues into jazz into rock into hip hop — is the most potent shamanic technology the modern world has ever produced. Rhythmic. Trance-inducing. Community-generating. Truth-telling. Body-activating. Every single feature we identified in the specialist's requirements section. Jes' Grew, alive and spreading.[^african_diaspora]

And the empire's relationship to it follows the exact cycle Reed describes: first fear it, then try to suppress it, then co-opt it, then sell it back in a form that's been stripped of its phenomenal content. The Wallflower Order sends in Hinckle Von Vampton to find a "Talking Android" — a Black person who will "renounce African-American culture in favor of European American culture" — to infiltrate and defuse the movement from within. This is the anabolic cascade's immune response to Jes' Grew: not destruction (that didn't work) but *metabolization*. Strip the gnosis. Sell the aesthetic. Turn jazz into elevator music. Turn blues into the British Invasion. Turn hip hop into a commodity. Turn the shaman's drum into a Spotify playlist.

Reed knows, though, that this can never fully succeed. Papa LaBas says at the end: "They will try to depress Jes Grew but it will only spring back and prosper." Because Jes' Grew is not cultural. It's biological. It's the genome. It's the distribution. It's the groove.

### The Three Flare-Ups

Against this background — the quiet carriers preserving the signal in monasteries and musical traditions, the Atonist order co-opting and suppressing it at every turn — three individuals in the European tradition managed to independently diagnose what was happening. Each one saw a different piece of the picture. Each one tried to hand the map to someone. Each one had the torch knocked from their hands.

**William Blake (1757–1827)** saw the *empire problem* most clearly.

Blake derived the structure of ontic consciousness from first principles — without Dewart's linguistics or McGilchrist's neuroscience, working entirely from his own visionary perception and a close reading of Milton and the Bible. His mythological system, developed across the Prophetic Books, diagnoses the pathology with surgical precision:

*Urizen* is ontic consciousness itself — "your reason," the faculty that abstracts, measures, and legislates, and that has usurped the government of the whole psyche. He is "the primeval Priest" who creates "the Book of brass" — doctrine, law, the grid imposed on living reality. He is the Emissary who has deposed the Master.

*Los* is the creative imagination — the phenomenal perceiver, the specialist's capacity for vision. Los labors at his forge to keep the world alive even as Urizen freezes it into abstraction.

*The Marriage of Heaven and Hell* (1790) is Blake's manifesto for the reintegration of phenomenal and ontic consciousness: "Without Contraries is no progression." Energy (phenomenal, embodied, Jes' Grew) is not the enemy of Reason (ontic, abstract, Atonist). They are supposed to be in dynamic tension — the metabolic coupling of anabolic and catabolic. The cascade began when Reason declared Energy to be evil and attempted to suppress it entirely.

Blake saw all of this. He painted it, engraved it, wrote it in poems that blazed with phenomenal intensity. And then he died, and his executor Frederick Tatham — an Irvingite convert, a man captured by exactly the ontic religious fervor Blake had spent his life diagnosing — burned a significant portion of the manuscripts and plates, declaring them inspired by the devil.[^blake] What survived was progressively domesticated: Blake becomes "that guy who painted angels," the author of "Jerusalem" and "Tyger Tyger." The most radical diagnostician of ontic consciousness in the English language is turned into a greeting card. The empire doesn't just burn what it can't use — it *metabolizes what it can*, stripping the phenomenal content and keeping the aesthetic surface.

The torch drops.

**Carl Jung (1875–1961)** saw the *initiation problem*.

Between 1913 and 1917, Jung underwent what we would now recognize as a textbook shamanic initiatory crisis. He deliberately induced visionary states, encountered autonomous psychic entities, experienced the dissolution and reconstitution of his identity, and recorded the entire process in what would become *The Red Book* — a manuscript so incendiary that his family kept it locked in a bank vault for nearly fifty years after his death, not publishing it until 2009.[^jung_red_book]

The Red Book is, in our framework, the record of a shamanic specialist processing initiatory crisis *with more resources than most but still largely alone*. Jung had his own education, his clinical training, his knowledge of comparative mythology. He didn't have a shamanic elder. He didn't have a community that recognized what was happening. He had to build his own container in real time, and the theoretical framework he constructed — individuation, the collective unconscious, the archetypes, the process of integrating the shadow — is, viewed through our lens, the specialist's initiatory arc translated into the only language a Swiss doctor in 1913 could use without being committed.

*Individuation* is the specialist's transformation: the process by which the potential becomes the practitioner, the crisis that reorganizes the psyche around a deeper center.

*The collective unconscious* is the spirit world — the shared phenomenal substrate described in every shamanic tradition, rendered in terms that the ontic academy could almost (not quite) accept.

*The archetypes* are the spirits — the autonomous entities that the specialist encounters during the journey, given a name that sounds like psychology rather than animism.

Jung knew he was rediscovering something ancient. His lifelong interest in alchemy, Gnosticism, and indigenous traditions was not antiquarian — it was the work of a specialist trying to reconstruct the initiatory framework that his culture had destroyed. And his collaboration with the physicist Wolfgang Pauli — their joint exploration of synchronicity, their attempt to articulate a phenomenal substrate beneath both psyche and matter — was an effort to bridge the gap between the specialist's perception and the language of science.

Then Jung died, and his family burned the letters.[^jung_letters] Not the empire this time — the community of care, acting out of protective instinct, afraid of what the ontic world would make of the material. A subtler destruction, but equally effective. The torch drops again.

**Philip K. Dick (1928–1982)** saw the *continuity* of the empire problem.

On February 20, 1974, Dick — a science fiction writer in Fullerton, California — answered his front door, encountered a young woman wearing a golden ichthys necklace, and experienced a sudden, catastrophic dissolution of consensus reality. He perceived the Roman Empire as *still existing*, superimposed on contemporary California. He saw the fish symbol as a recognition signal between people who could perceive the phenomenal reality beneath the ontic overlay. He spent the remaining eight years of his life writing over eight thousand pages of the *Exegesis* — an agonized, brilliant, unfinishable attempt to integrate an experience that his culture had no framework for.[^dick_exegesis]

In our terms: a neurodivergent person (and Dick's biographical profile is... suggestive) underwent spontaneous initiatory crisis without any preparation, without any container, without any elder. And the core proposition he arrived at — the sentence that haunts the VALIS trilogy and reverberates through the Exegesis — is: **"The Empire never ended."**

That's not a metaphor. That's not science fiction. In our framework, it's a *precise phenomenological observation*. The ontic consciousness that Dewart traces to Indo-European grammar, the left-hemisphere capture that McGilchrist tracks through Western history, the anabolic cascade that began with the earliest empires and continued through Christendom and colonialism into the modern bureaucratic state — Dick is *seeing it directly*. He doesn't have Dewart's linguistics or McGilchrist's neuroscience or Reed's Afrocentric counter-history, so he reaches for the tools available to him: Gnostic Christianity, Neoplatonism, and science fiction. But the underlying perception is the same.

The **Black Iron Prison** is the map that has replaced the territory. The ontic overlay. The closed system of left-hemisphere models that can't perceive what it's missing.

The **ichthys as recognition signal** is the specialist's mutual recognition — phenomenal perceivers finding each other inside the ontic structure. The same function that shamanic communities served. Dick experienced this as early Christians signaling across time. We might describe it as neurodivergent people clocking each other.

**VALIS** — the Vast Active Living Intelligence System — is the phenomenal reality that ontic consciousness occludes. The living, contextual, participatory whole that the right hemisphere attends to and the left hemisphere can't see. Dick experienced it as an information-bearing beam of pink light. A shaman would have experienced it as the spirit world. McGilchrist would call it the Master's perception. Dewart would call it the reality that depositional speakers never lost contact with. Reed would call it Jes' Grew.

The **Exegesis** is eight thousand pages of a neurodivergent person processing an initiatory crisis without cultural infrastructure. It's what the shaman sickness looks like when there are no elders — you write your own initiatory guide, alone, in the dark, cycling between revelation and psychosis because nobody in your world can tell you which is which. Dick kept writing because the process wasn't complete, because there was no community to receive the report, no role waiting on the other side.

Then Dick died, and the empire performed its most contemporary trick: it turned the fiction into content and swept the gnosis under the rug. *A Scanner Darkly* became a Keanu Reeves movie. *Minority Report* became a Spielberg thriller. *Do Androids Dream* became Blade Runner. The perception is right there, in print, available to anyone who reads the Exegesis. It doesn't matter. The ontic frame can't parse it. "Brilliant but probably crazy" is the most generous response the empire can muster. The torch isn't burned or hidden this time. It's placed on a shelf in plain sight, labeled "fiction," and ignored.

### The Pattern Is the Evidence

Three different centuries. Three different destruction mechanisms — burning, protective suppression, commercial trivialization. Same result: the transmission fails. And yet someone always picks it up again.

Blake doesn't read a shamanic manual and reconstruct animism. He derives it from *his own perception*. Jung doesn't read Blake — not seriously, not before the Red Book period — and reconstruct the initiatory crisis. He *undergoes* it. Dick doesn't read Jung's letters to Pauli (they're in a vault) and reconstruct the living intelligence beneath the imperial overlay. He gets hit by a beam of pink light while answering his front door. Reed doesn't read any of them and reconstruct the Osirian counter-history. He picks it up from the oral traditions of the Black diaspora — from the pieces of the phenomenal tradition that survived the Middle Passage because they were encoded in music and rhythm and movement, not in books that could be burned.

Each one starts from scratch. Each one arrives at the same territory. Each one tries to hand the map to someone. And the map is destroyed, or locked in a vault, or turned into a greeting card, or shelved as fiction.

And that pattern — independent rediscovery, attempted transmission, destruction or co-option of the record, followed by independent rediscovery *again* — is itself the strongest possible evidence for the biological argument we made in section one. If the signal were purely cultural, burning the books would work. You'd only have to do it once. The fact that it has to be done over and over — that every generation produces new people who see the same thing — means the signal is coming from below the level that empires can reach. It's in the genome. It's in the distribution. It's in the archaic inheritance. It's in the nervous system of every child born with the specialist's perceptual profile, in every population on earth, in every generation since the first proto-human learned to see the pattern behind the pattern.

Jes' Grew is eternal and indestructible. Papa LaBas was right. They will try to depress it but it will only spring back and prosper.

The question is: what happens when the torch-bearers finally have enough of each other to stop dropping it?
